Sales CRM Knowledge Graph Ontology - V1
=======================================

Purpose
-------
The goal is to build a system that combines structured CRM data and sales/marketing transcripts into a Knowledge Graph that an LLM can use to answer questions such as:

- How can I close this deal?
- How can I increase the value / sales volume of this deal?


1. Why the ontology is not derived only from the CSV columns
------------------------------------------------------------

The CRM CSV files already give us some obvious concepts.

From the example datasets:

accounts.csv
- Account
- Industry

contacts.csv
- Person / CustomerContact
- Account

deals.csv
- Deal
- Product
- SalesRep
- DealStage

activities.csv
- Interaction
- Call
- Meeting
- Email

transcripts.csv
- Transcript

So the CSV structure is useful and gives us the initial entities.

However, the CSV schema only describes how the CRM stores data. It does not contain all the semantic concepts required to answer higher-level sales questions.

For example, a transcript may contain:

"Procurement expects a better price."

There is no "blocker" column in deals.csv, but semantically we may want to represent:

Deal D001 --HAS_BLOCKER--> Pricing

Similarly, transcripts may reveal concepts such as:

- Blocker
- Objection
- CustomerNeed
- PainPoint
- BusinessGoal
- DecisionCriterion
- BuyingSignal
- Risk
- Competitor
- SalesAction
- ExpansionOpportunity

These concepts cannot be derived only from the CRM column names.


2. Why competency questions are useful
---------------------------------------

Before defining the ontology, we define the questions the system should be able to answer.

These are called competency questions.

They help us determine which concepts and relationships are actually necessary.

Example competency questions:

1. What are the main blockers preventing this deal from closing?
2. Who are the key decision makers and influencers?
3. What customer needs or business goals are driving the deal?
4. What objections or concerns has the customer raised?
5. What actions have already been taken to address those objections?
6. What next actions are most likely to help close the deal?
7. Which previously won deals had similar blockers or needs?
8. What actions helped those similar deals close?
9. What additional products or services could be relevant?
10. What evidence suggests that the customer could expand the number of users, locations, products, or contract duration?
11. What risks could cause the deal to be lost or delayed?

The point is that every competency question forces us to ask:

"What concepts and relationships must exist in the graph for this question to be answerable?"

For example:

Question:
What are the blockers preventing this deal from closing?

Required concepts:
- Deal
- Blocker

Required relationship:
Deal --HAS_BLOCKER--> Blocker

Question:
What worked in similar won deals?

Required concepts:
- Deal
- Blocker
- SalesAction
- DealOutcome

Required relationships:
Deal --HAS_BLOCKER--> Blocker
Deal --HAS_ACTION--> SalesAction
SalesAction --ADDRESSES--> Blocker
Deal --HAS_OUTCOME--> DealOutcome


3. Derivation process
---------------------

The ontology was derived in four steps.

Step 1 - Start from the CRM schema

Use the CSV files to identify the obvious operational entities:

- Deal
- Account
- Person
- SalesRep
- Product
- Interaction
- Transcript
- Industry

Step 2 - Define competency questions

Define the actual business questions the LLM needs to answer.

The two main goals are:

A. Close the deal
This requires understanding:
- blockers
- objections
- stakeholders
- risks
- customer needs
- next actions
- similar historical deals

B. Increase deal value
This requires understanding:
- buying signals
- customer needs
- expansion opportunities
- additional products
- more users / locations
- longer contract duration
- cross-sell / upsell possibilities

Step 3 - Identify concepts missing from the CSV schema

From transcripts and competency questions we introduce:

- CustomerNeed
- BusinessGoal
- PainPoint
- Objection
- Blocker
- Risk
- DecisionCriterion
- BuyingSignal
- Competitor
- SalesAction
- ExpansionOpportunity
- UpsellOpportunity
- CrossSellOpportunity
- ScopeExpansion
- ContractExpansion
- DealOutcome
- Evidence

Step 4 - Define the relationships needed to answer the questions

The relationships below form the V1 ontology.


4. Ontology V1
--------------

4.1 Core CRM concepts

Classes:

- Account
- Person
    - CustomerContact
    - SalesRep
- Deal
- Product
- Interaction

    - Email

4.3 Deal outcome and historical learning

Deal properties may include:

- stage
- status
- dealValue
- winProbability
- expectedCloseDate
- lossReason

Class:

DealOutcome
    - Won
    - Lost
    - Open

Relationships:

Deal --HAS_OUTCOME--> DealOutcome

SalesAction --CONTRIBUTED_TO--> DealOutcome

The purpose of this layer is to learn from historical deals.

Example:

Find historical deals that:
- had blocker = Security
- ended in outcome = Won

Then retrieve:
- which SalesActions addressed Security

This can help answer:

"How can I close D001?"


4.4 Evidence and provenance

Because many graph facts are extracted from unstructured transcripts by an LLM, we should keep evidence for those facts.

Class:

Evidence

Relationships:

Blocker --SUPPORTED_BY--> Evidence
CustomerNeed --SUPPORTED_BY--> Evidence
BuyingSignal --SUPPORTED_BY--> Evidence
DecisionCriterion --SUPPORTED_BY--> Evidence

Evidence may contain:

- sourceTranscript
- sourceInteraction
- speaker
- textExcerpt
- timestamp
- extractionConfidence

Example:

D001 --HAS_BLOCKER--> Pricing

Pricing --SUPPORTED_BY--> Evidence E123

Evidence E123:
- transcript = T001
- speaker = Anna Keller
- excerpt = "procurement expects a better price..."
- confidence = 0.94

This is important because an LLM-extracted fact should remain traceable back to its source.


5. Example: Deal D001
---------------------

A simplified representation of D001 could look like:

D001 --FOR_ACCOUNT--> Acme Manufacturing
D001 --INVOLVES_PRODUCT--> Enterprise Platform

D001 --HAS_BLOCKER--> Pricing
D001 --HAS_BLOCKER--> Security

Pricing --RAISED_BY--> Procurement
Security --RAISED_BY--> Markus Vogel

D001 --HAS_ACTION--> Three-year pricing option
Three-year pricing option --ADDRESSES--> Pricing

D001 --HAS_BUYING_SIGNAL--> Three-factory deployment

D001 --HAS_EXPANSION_OPPORTUNITY--> ScopeExpansion


6. How the graph supports the two main business questions
---------------------------------------------------------

Question 1:
How can I close D001?

Possible graph traversal:

D001
-> blockers
-> similar historical deals
-> actions used to address those blockers
-> outcomes of those deals
-> actions associated with successful outcomes

Example:

D001
-> HAS_BLOCKER -> Security
-> find other deals with Security blocker
-> find deals with outcome Won
-> retrieve SalesActions that addressed Security

The LLM can then summarize those findings into a recommendation.


Question 2:
How can I increase the value of D001?

Possible graph traversal:

D001
-> customer needs
-> buying signals
-> expansion opportunities
-> additional products / locations / users / contract duration
-> similar successful expansions

Example:

D001
-> HAS_BUYING_SIGNAL -> Three-factory deployment
-> HAS_EXPANSION_OPPORTUNITY -> ScopeExpansion

The LLM can use this to recommend expanding the deal from one factory to three factories.


7. Design principle for V1
--------------------------

The ontology should remain intentionally limited.

We should not add concepts simply because they sound useful.

A new class or relationship should normally be added when:

1. a competency question requires it, or
2. the source data reveals an important recurring concept that the current ontology cannot represent.

This avoids building an unnecessarily large and difficult-to-maintain ontology.

Summary
-------

The ontology was derived by combining two sources:

1. CRM schema
   -> gives us the obvious operational entities.

2. Competency questions + transcripts
   -> reveal the semantic concepts that are not present in the CRM schema.

The final flow is:

CSV schema
    ->
initial CRM concepts
    ->
competency questions
    ->
missing semantic concepts
    ->
relationships
    ->
ontology
    ->
LLM + Knowledge Graph reasoning

The resulting V1 ontology focuses on representing:

- deals
- customers
- stakeholders
- products
- interactions
- needs
- blockers
- objections
- risks
- buying signals
- sales actions
- expansion opportunities
- outcomes
- evidence / provenance

This is enough to begin answering the two core questions:

"How can I close this deal?"

and

"How can I increase the sales volume/value of this deal?"
